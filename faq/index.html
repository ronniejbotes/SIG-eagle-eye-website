About Eagle Eye

What is SIG Eagle Eye?

A proactive labour intelligence and compliance service developed by labour law specialists. It combines AI-powered behavioural analytics with labour-law-aligned classification, human review and structured evidence reporting, so employers can deal with misconduct, safety breaches and poor performance early and fairly.

How is it different from ordinary CCTV?

CCTV records everything and leaves you with hours of footage and no case. Eagle Eye classifies what matters against labour law and your own disciplinary code, sends it to a person for review, and produces a structured record you can act on. See how it works.

Does the system decide who gets disciplined?

No. It flags, classifies and documents. A person reviews every incident, and management decides what happens next, following a fair procedure.

Which industries is it configured for?

Manufacturing, logistics and fleet, mining and earthmoving, healthcare facilities, retail and warehousing, and security operations. Each site is then configured around its own zones, rules and disciplinary code. Find your industry.

Legal & privacy

Is it legal to monitor employees with cameras in South Africa?

Generally yes, in work areas, when employees know about it, the purpose is legitimate and the information is handled in line with POPIA. Private spaces such as toilets and change rooms are off limits. This is general information, not legal advice. See the compliance framework.

Do we need employees' consent to monitor them?

Consent is one lawful basis under POPIA, but not the only one. Section 11 also allows processing that is necessary for the employment contract, to comply with a legal obligation such as health and safety, or for your legitimate interests. Whatever the basis, employees must be told what is collected and why.

Does POPIA allow AI to make decisions about employees?

Not on its own. Section 71 of POPIA says a person may not be subject to a decision with legal consequences, or that affects them substantially, based solely on automated processing that profiles things like their performance at work or conduct. That is why a person reviews every flag and management makes every decision.

Will CCTV footage be accepted at the CCMA?

Arbitrators have accepted video evidence when it is clear, shown to be authentic and unaltered, and lawfully obtained, and rejected it when it was not. A reviewed clip kept with its record and an access log is built for that test, although no one can promise an outcome.

Are toilets, change rooms or fitting rooms ever monitored?

Never. Private spaces are excluded by design. At most, the entrance to an area such as a fitting-room corridor can be observed where your policy allows it.

Who is responsible under POPIA, the employer or SIG Eagle Eye?

Generally the employer is the responsible party for its employees' information, and SIG Eagle Eye processes it on the employer's behalf under a written agreement, as POPIA's rules for operators in sections 20 and 21 require.

Do we have to involve our union?

Collective agreements take precedence over the Dismissal Code, so check yours first. Where you have a recognised union, consult it before monitoring goes live. It is also the fastest way to build trust on the floor.

How it works

Do we need new cameras?

Not necessarily. The site assessment starts with the coverage you already have and tells you honestly where it falls short.

Who reviews the flags?

Reviewers are agreed at setup, so review sits with people who know your rules. SIG Eagle Eye's labour law specialists support the process, and optional legal integration support is available when an incident moves into a formal procedure.

Can we choose what is and isn't monitored?

Yes. That is what configuration is. You choose the zones and the rules within what the law allows. Private spaces are always excluded.

How long are records kept?

Only as long as the purpose needs and your policy and the law require. After that they are deleted, in line with POPIA's rule on retention.

What happens when the system gets something wrong?

A person reviews every flag, so false alarms are dismissed before they reach anyone, and the detection is tuned so the same mistake does not keep happening.

For employees

Will our employees know about it?

Yes. Transparent employee notification is one of the built-in safeguards, and it never operates secretly. Our page for employees explains it in plain language you can share with your team.

Can an employee refuse to be monitored?

Monitoring at work is generally lawful when it is limited to work areas, has a legitimate purpose and employees have been told about it. Employees with concerns can raise them with the employer, their union or the Information Officer, and can complain to the Information Regulator if they believe their information is mishandled.

Will a flag go on an employee's record?

A flag is not a disciplinary record. Only an issue that a person has reviewed and confirmed goes onto a record, and even then the first step is usually a conversation.

Can employees see what is recorded about them?

Yes. Under POPIA sections 23 and 24, employees can ask what personal information is held about them and ask for it to be corrected.

Setup & pricing

How does pricing work?

It is a modular subscription. After a site assessment we recommend the modules your operation needs and quote per site, so a single store and a multi-site group each pay for what they use.

What happens at a site assessment?

We walk your site with you: the zones, the risks, your existing camera coverage, your disciplinary code and the rules people already sign for. You get a recommendation of the modules you need and a quote per site. Book one here.

How long does it take to go live?

It depends on the number of sites and how ready your policies are. The site assessment gives you a clear plan and timeline before you commit.

Does it work across several sites?

Yes. It scales across multiple sites, with rules configured per site and reporting site by site or across the whole group.
