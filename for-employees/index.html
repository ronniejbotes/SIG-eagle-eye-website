What SIG Eagle Eye means for you at work.

If your employer uses SIG Eagle Eye, you deserve to know exactly what it does. This page explains it in plain language: what it looks at, what it never does, what happens if something is flagged, and the rights you keep.

Your rights If something is flagged

“You'll always be told what is monitored, and why.”

What you may have heard

Six worries. Six straight answers.

Tap a card to turn it over.

Where it looks

Work areas only. Never your private spaces.

What it can look at, if your employer's policy says so

  • Work floors, production lines and warehouses
  • Loading bays, yards, gates and entrances
  • Stores, stock rooms and medicine rooms
  • Till points and cash offices
  • Security posts and patrol routes

What it never looks at

  • Toilets, change rooms and showers
  • Patient rooms and treatment areas
  • Anywhere outside the agreed work areas
  • Your life outside work

If something is flagged about you

A flag is a question. You get to answer it.

Here is what actually happens, step by step. Most flags never get past step two.

  1. 01

    A flag is raised

    The system notices something that matches a workplace rule. That is all a flag is: a reason for someone to look.

  2. 02

    A person reviews it

    Most flags end here: a false alarm, a good reason, or nothing worth raising. A dismissed flag doesn't count against you.

  3. 03

    A conversation

    If it matters, the first step is usually a talk with your supervisor, to sort it out early and informally.

  4. 04

    If it becomes formal

    You must be told what the allegation is, in writing and in a language you understand, given time to prepare, and a real chance to respond. You can bring a fellow employee or your union representative.

  5. 05

    You keep your rights

    If you are dismissed and believe it was unfair, you can refer a dispute to the CCMA or your bargaining council, generally within 30 days of the dismissal.

Your rights

What stays yours, whatever the technology.

These come from the Constitution and the laws that protect every employee in South Africa. No system, and no employer, can switch them off.

  1. 1

    Privacy

    Monitoring must stay limited to work purposes, and private spaces are off limits.

    Constitution, section 14
  2. 2

    To be told

    What information is collected about you, why, and who is responsible for it.

    POPIA, section 18
  3. 3

    To see it and correct it

    You can ask what is held about you, and ask for anything wrong to be corrected.

    POPIA, sections 23 and 24
  4. 4

    Not to be judged by a machine alone

    No decision with legal consequences may rest only on automated processing of your conduct or performance.

    POPIA, section 71
  5. 5

    A fair reason and a fair procedure

    Before any dismissal: a fair reason, a known rule, and your chance to respond.

    LRA, section 188 · Dismissal Code
  6. 6

    To be represented

    At a disciplinary enquiry, by a fellow employee or a union representative.

    Code of Good Practice: Dismissal
  7. 7

    Equal treatment

    The same rules for everyone, with no unfair discrimination in how they are applied.

    EEA, section 6
  8. 8

    A safe place to work

    Your employer must keep the workplace safe. On a mine, you may leave a place of serious danger.

    OHSA, section 8 · MHSA, section 23

Why it can work for you

Judged on facts, not on favour.

Most people come to work to do a good job. A consistent record is on their side.

  • Same rulesThe rule that applies to you applies to everyone, whoever they are and whoever they know.
  • Your nameWhen something goes missing or goes wrong, the record shows who wasn't involved, too.
  • Safer shiftsUnsafe acts and hazards are picked up before someone gets hurt.
  • Early fixesProblems are raised while a conversation can still sort them out, long before a hearing.

Your checklist

Questions to ask your employer.

You are entitled to clear answers. Take these to your supervisor, HR, your union representative or the Information Officer.

  1. What exactly is monitored, and why?
  2. Which areas are covered, and which are never covered?
  3. Who reviews flags, and who can see the records?
  4. How long are records kept?
  5. How do I ask to see information about me?
  6. Who is the Information Officer?
  7. Where can I read the monitoring policy?

Employees ask

Your questions

All questions
Can I refuse to be monitored at work?

Monitoring at work is generally lawful when it is limited to work areas, has a legitimate purpose and you have been told about it. If you have concerns, raise them with your employer, your union or the Information Officer. If you believe your personal information is being mishandled, you can also complain to the Information Regulator.

Will a flag go on my record?

A flag is not a disciplinary record. Only an issue that a person has reviewed and confirmed goes onto a record, and even then the first step is usually a conversation.

What if the system gets it wrong?

That is exactly why a person reviews every flag. Wrong readings are dismissed and the system is tuned. If information about you is still wrong, you can ask for it to be corrected.

Who can I talk to about it?

Your supervisor, HR, your union representative or your employer's Information Officer.
