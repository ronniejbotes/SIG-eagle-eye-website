Logistics & fleet

Every bay, every load, every handover.

Loads rarely go missing on the highway alone. They get short-loaded at the bay, swapped in the yard or driven out after hours. SIG Eagle Eye gives your distribution centres and depots a reviewed chain of custody, so you know what left, when and with whom.

trucks hijacked in South Africa in the second quarter of 2025 alone, according to SAPS crime statistics.

As reported by Aon South Africa, 2026.

The road gets the headlines. The yard gets the inside job.

Aon's review of South African cargo risk names insider enablement, meaning compromised or corrupt employees with access to schedules, routes and cargo details, as a critical enabler of freight crime.

You can't put a camera on every kilometre. You can make sure that what happens at your bays, gates and depots is seen, classified and reviewed, and that the people who do things properly can prove it.

Chain of custody

Follow one load from pick to return.

Five points where loads go wrong, and what Eagle Eye records at each one.

  1. 01 · Distribution centre

    Pick & stage

    Orders are picked and staged at the bay. Eagle Eye notes when staging starts, which pallets go where, and whether anyone who shouldn't be in the staging lane is handling stock.

    AccessStaging times

  2. 02 · Loading bay

    Count what goes on

    Pallets and cases loaded are counted against the manifest as they go into the trailer. A short or over load is flagged while the truck is still at the bay, not three days later at the customer.

    Load vs manifestForklift safety

  3. 03 · Gate

    Seal and sign out

    The seal goes on, the gate records the exit time. A trailer that leaves unsealed, leaves early or stops in the yard to take on something extra is exactly the kind of event that goes to human review.

    Seal & exitHuman review

  4. 04 · Depot & yard

    Overnight is when it happens

    Vehicles moved after hours, fuel drawn outside refuelling windows, trailers opened in the yard. The depot is quiet at night, which is why patterns show up so clearly.

    After-hours useFuel bay

  5. 05 · Return

    Returns and offloads

    Returned stock, damages and empty pallets are counted back in. Returns are a common route for stock to disappear, because nobody expects them to be checked.

    Returns countDamages

Configured for your operation

Where logistics loses money quietly.

Each flag is classified against labour law and your own rules before a person reviews it.

Load integrity

Short loads, over loads, swapped stock and trailers opened after sealing. The count is recorded when it happens, so disputes start from evidence.

Yard & dock safety

Forklifts near pedestrians, people behind reversing trucks, speeding in the yard and loading without wheel chocks. Unsafe acts recorded before they become injuries.

Vehicle & fuel misuse

Company vehicles moved after hours, fuel drawn outside set windows and trailers parked where they shouldn't be. Patterns, not guesswork.

Turnaround & dwell time

Trucks waiting at bays, teams idle between loads, loading that stretches into overtime. Measured so you can fix the schedule as well as the shift.

When a customer says the load arrived short, the driver who loaded it properly deserves proof.

Eagle Eye's records work in both directions. They show where a problem started, and they clear the people who had nothing to do with it.

In the courts

When the dashcam has the final word.

Road transport has some of the clearest case law on camera evidence. In these four judgments, footage, the facts of a handover and the way a rule was applied decided who was right.

  1. Labour Court · 2025

    EmployerLost

    The blurred rule.

    What happened
    A car-rental supervisor employed since 2010 used a company car, without a manager's approval, to help a motorist with a flat tyre near home during a late shift, and was dismissed.
    What decided it
    The CCMA found the dismissal unfair. In December 2025 the Labour Court agreed: the rule existed on paper, but the line between allowed and forbidden use had been blurred in practice by both sides. The employee was reinstated.
    What a reviewed record adds
    Vehicle-use rules logged and applied the same way every time, so what's allowed never drifts into habit.
    Judgment: [2025] ZALCJHB 587

  2. Labour Court · 2025

    EmployerWonon review

    Two trucks, one double line.

    What happened
    A truck's camera recorded the driver, with no seatbelt, overtaking two trucks across a double barrier line late at night, and causing a crash. He was dismissed.
    What decided it
    The arbitrator found the footage didn't show the manoeuvre and awarded him R80,400, six months' pay. The Labour Court found the footage directly contradicted that finding and set the award aside in August 2025.
    What a reviewed record adds
    The clip, the time and the route filed together, so the evidence is easy to follow and hard to overlook.
    Judgment: [2025] ZALCJHB 364

  3. Labour Appeal Court · 2024

    EmployerWonon appeal

    Red means stop.

    What happened
    A bus driver collided with a minibus taxi on a route she knew well. The bus's own camera showed her speeding up on amber and going through a red light.
    What decided it
    The arbitrator upheld the dismissal, the Labour Court overturned it and reinstated her, and the Labour Appeal Court restored it on the totality of the evidence.
    What a reviewed record adds
    Evidence that stands on its own at every stage, from the disciplinary hearing to an appeal.
    Judgment: [2024] ZALAC 42

  4. Labour Court · 2019

    EmployeeCleared

    The last pallet.

    What happened
    A refrigerated-truck driver was dismissed after his truck's door hinges were damaged while it was offloaded at an airport cargo terminal.
    What decided it
    The forklift unloaded 22 pallets without harming the hinges and damaged them on the last one, so the driver hadn't caused it. Reinstated with six months' back pay; the employer's review was dismissed.
    What a reviewed record adds
    A record of the handover itself, so damage is put down to the right person on the day.
    Judgment: [2019] ZALCJHB 165

The lessonClear footage and clearly applied rules decided all four. Where either was missing, the employer lost, sometimes years later.

Operations managers ask

Logistics questions

All questions
Do you monitor drivers while they're on the road?

Eagle Eye is configured for your fixed sites: distribution centres, loading bays, yards, fuel points and depots. In-cab cameras and vehicle tracking are separate systems. If you already run them, how they fit in is discussed at the site assessment.

Can it help settle short-delivery disputes?

It helps you know what left your bay. A reviewed, time-stamped record of what was loaded and sealed gives you something firmer than two conflicting counts when a customer reports a shortage.

Will it stop hijackings?

No, and we won't claim it does. Eagle Eye is not a tracking or armed-response service. It helps inside your sites: patterns of loading irregularities, after-hours vehicle movement and access to areas where loads and paperwork are kept.

We run night shifts and weekend loading. Is that covered?

Yes. Out-of-hours activity is often where the most useful patterns are, and reviews are scheduled around your operating hours.
