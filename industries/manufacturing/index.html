The problem with the factory floor

Nothing costly happens only once.

An injury on a press line usually follows months of the same shortcut. Stock loss follows a route someone has used before. Late returns from breaks become normal because nobody wrote the first ones down.

Supervisors see some of it, remember less and record even less. When it finally reaches a disciplinary hearing, the evidence is thin and the employee's first defence is that everybody does it. Eagle Eye gives you the record you were missing: consistent, classified and reviewed by a person.

What it is configured to catch

Machine safety & PPE

Guards and interlocks bypassed, hands inside danger zones, eye, ear and foot protection missing, hot work without the right kit. The Occupational Health and Safety Act puts the duty to provide a safe workplace on you, and the duty to follow safety rules on every employee. Eagle Eye helps you show both.

Why classification matters

Misconduct or poor performance? The CCMA will ask.

Treat a skills problem as a disciplinary offence and a fair dismissal can still be found unfair. The Code of Good Practice: Dismissal handles the two very differently.

Eagle Eye keeps them apart from the first record, so the process you follow matches the problem you have.

Misconduct

A known rule, broken

The operator knew the guard must stay closed and tied it open anyway.

  • Was there a rule, and did they know it?
  • Is it applied consistently?
  • Counselling, warnings, then a hearing

Poor performance

A standard, not yet met

The operator is trying, and still falls short of the line's standard.

  • Was the standard clear and reasonable?
  • Evaluation, instruction, training, guidance
  • A fair chance to improve, on record

In the courts

The evidence showed less than the charge claimed.

Two Labour Court cases from South African factories. In both, the employer had a camera or a photo, and in both it showed less than the charge said.

  1. Labour Court · reported 2026

    EmployerLost

    One grainy photograph.

    What happened
    A tyre maker dismissed three shop stewards during a strike, on charges including inciting an unprotected strike, locking the main gate and damaging its name in a radio interview.
    What decided it
    The only evidence of the locked gate was a grainy photo in which nobody could be identified, backed by a single witness, and a steward who posted an inflammatory WhatsApp message had only been briefly suspended. The commissioner reinstated all three with 21 months' back pay, and the Labour Court upheld the award.
    What a reviewed record adds
    Clear footage that identifies who did what, reviewed on the day, and a record of how similar conduct was handled.
    Report: [2026] 5 BLLR 495 (LC)

  2. Labour Appeal Court · 2014

    EmployerLoston sanction

    A few sips.

    What happened
    Beer kept going missing from a brewery's sample fridges, so the company installed a hidden camera. It recorded a process operator drinking from a bottle before going back to operating heavy machinery. He was dismissed.
    What decided it
    The footage showed “a few sips”, not the whole bottle, and a breathalyser test found no alcohol. Weighing his long service, the commissioner found the misconduct proven but dismissal too harsh, and ordered reinstatement without back pay. The Labour Appeal Court agreed.
    What a reviewed record adds
    Clips that capture the whole act, and a record of how similar cases were handled, so the sanction fits what the evidence shows.
    Judgment: [2014] ZALAC 26

The lessonEvidence has to identify the person and show the whole act, and the sanction has to fit what it shows.

Where it looks, and where it never does

Your floor, zone by zone.

Pick an area of this example plant to see what Eagle Eye is set up to notice there. Change rooms and toilets are never monitored.

Press line · Monitored

Guarding, exclusion zones and PPE

  • Guards, light curtains and interlocks left open or bypassed
  • Hands or bodies inside marked danger zones
  • Eye, hearing and foot protection

Assembly lines · Monitored

Flow, idle stations and PPE

  • Stations left unattended during production
  • Line stoppages and bottlenecks, as trends
  • Gloves and glasses where the task requires them

Stores cage · Monitored

Access and issuing

  • Entry by people who are not authorised for the cage
  • Tools and materials issued without a requisition
  • Out-of-hours access

Dispatch & loading · Monitored

Loads, forklifts and people

  • Quantities loaded against the dispatch note
  • Forklift and pedestrian interaction
  • Loading outside of scheduled times

Side gate · Monitored

What leaves, and how

  • Goods carried out without paperwork
  • Private vehicles parked at non-delivery points
  • Gate left open or unmanned

Canteen · Entrance only

Break times, nothing more

Only the doorway is used, to measure when breaks start and end. What people do, say or eat on their break is their own business.

Change rooms & toilets · Never monitored

Off limits. Always.

Private spaces are never monitored. It is unlawful in principle and it would destroy the trust the whole system depends on.

Plant managers ask

Manufacturing questions

All questions
Can it tell the difference between misconduct and poor performance on a line?

It classifies them separately. Breaking a known rule, such as bypassing a machine guard, is treated as possible misconduct. Falling short of output while trying is treated as a performance matter, which calls for evaluation, training and guidance first. A person reviews every classification before anything happens.

Does monitoring break times conflict with the BCEA?

No. Eagle Eye measures against the meal intervals and breaks your employees are entitled to and have agreed to. It flags overruns. It never shortens anyone's lawful break.

Should we consult our union before switching it on?

We recommend it. Explaining what is monitored, why, and what is left alone, before go-live, builds trust and lets you check your collective agreements for any consultation duties.

Will it work with the cameras we already have?

Existing coverage is reviewed during the site assessment. We map which zones are already covered, where a view or position needs to change, and whether anything needs to be added.
