A shift at till 4, printed as a review slip.

Most till fraud isn't dramatic. It's a quiet routine: a void here, a refund there, the drawer opened with no sale. On its own, each one has an innocent explanation. Together, they have a shape.

Eagle Eye lines up what happened at the till with the rules you already have, and hands the pattern to a person to review. The cashier who does it properly never appears on the slip at all.

From the front of the store to the back of the DC

Five places stock goes missing.

Till points

Voids, refunds, no-sale drawer openings, overrides and staff processing their own purchases. Each has a legitimate use, which is why the pattern matters more than the single event.

  • Refunds and voids with no customer present
  • Drawer opened without a sale
  • Staff purchases rung up on their own till

Stock room

High-value lines, damaged stock write-offs and the corner of the stock room that the camera never quite covered. Access and handling are recorded where losses actually happen.

  • Entry by staff not assigned to the stock room
  • Damaged or expired stock removed without a write-off
  • Bags and personal items in stock areas

Receiving

Deliveries signed for without a count, suppliers' staff unaccompanied in the back of house, and cartons that arrive but never reach the floor.

  • Goods received without a count or a second signature
  • Delivery crews in areas they shouldn't be
  • Returns to vendor that don't match paperwork

Warehouse & DC

Pick and pack lines, forklift traffic, mezzanines and cages. Productivity and safety are measured together, because a rushed pick line is also an unsafe one.

  • Forklift and pedestrian interaction in aisles
  • Idle stations and bottlenecks on pick lines
  • Access to high-value cages

Opening & closing

The first and last hour are when there are fewest people and most opportunity. Doors, alarms and cash-up routines are recorded as they happen.

  • Back doors opened or propped outside delivery times
  • Cash-up done alone when policy needs two
  • Staff remaining after the store is locked

Never inside fitting rooms, toilets or change rooms. Private spaces are excluded. At most, the entrance to a fitting-room area can be observed where your policy allows it.

In the courts

The camera was there. Nobody watched.

Retail brings about 1 in 8 of all CCMA referrals, year after year. In each of these three judgments the store had CCTV, and still couldn't prove its case.

  1. Labour Court · 2026

    EmployerLost

    Suspicion is not proof.

    What happened
    A departmental coordinator with 16 years' service was dismissed after CCTV showed her behaving “suspiciously” in a high-risk stockroom.
    What decided it
    No item was seen being hidden and no loss was recorded. The court upheld her reinstatement with back pay: suspicion, however strong, cannot replace proof.
    What a reviewed record adds
    A reviewer who asks the court's question first: what does this footage actually show?
    Judgment: [2026] ZALCJHB 50

  2. Labour Court · 2024

    EmployerLost

    A spoonful of sugar.

    What happened
    A baker employed since 1991 was charged with consuming company stock after CCTV showed him stirring sugar into a mug.
    What decided it
    The clip never showed him drinking it, so consumption wasn't proved. The CCMA reinstated him with back pay, and the Labour Court dismissed the employer's review.
    What a reviewed record adds
    A clip of the whole act, start to finish, and a reviewer who checks what it proves before a charge is laid.
    Judgment: [2024] ZALCPE 36

  3. Labour Appeal Court · 2023

    EmployerLost

    The footage nobody watched.

    What happened
    Stock losses at a hardware branch climbed from 0.47% to 3.63% of sales in the first half of 2016. After final warnings, all 12 staff were dismissed for collective misconduct.
    What decided it
    CCTV footage was available, but nobody reviewed it, because of the time it would take. With no one linked to the losses, the Labour Appeal Court ordered all 12 reinstated in 2023, with back pay from the day they were dismissed.
    What a reviewed record adds
    Footage reviewed as it's recorded, so a pattern at the receiving door reaches a named manager long before the stock count.
    Judgment: [2023] ZALAC 8

The lessonAll three stores had cameras. None had a reviewed record of the act itself, and that's the gap Eagle Eye is built to close.

Loss prevention managers ask

Retail questions

All questions
Is this a shoplifting detection system?

No. It is focused on your own workplace: staff conduct, procedures and safety at tills, stock rooms, receiving and warehouse aisles. Customer-facing loss prevention is a different discipline.

Can it link what happens at the till to our POS data?

What can be connected depends on your systems and is scoped at the site assessment. The principle stays the same either way: a flag is reviewed by a person before it becomes an allegation.

Are fitting rooms monitored?

Never inside. Private spaces such as fitting rooms, toilets and changing rooms are excluded. At most, the entrance to a fitting-room area can be observed where your policy allows it.

Will a warning based on Eagle Eye hold up at the CCMA?

Nobody can promise an outcome. What Eagle Eye gives you is what arbitrators look for: a clear, consistent record of what happened, a known rule, and evidence that it was applied the same way to everyone.
