Fatigue or misconduct?

The roster is evidence too.

An officer asleep at 03:00 might be breaking post orders. They might also be on a schedule that breaks the rules on rest. Eagle Eye shows you the pattern, so the decision you make is the right one, and it holds up if it is challenged.

BCEA · Sections 15 and 17

What the law says about night shifts

Night work means work after 18:00 and before 06:00. It needs the employee's agreement, an allowance or fewer working hours, and transport between home and work at the start and end of the shift.

An employer who regularly needs someone to work between 23:00 and 06:00 must tell them, in a language they understand, about the health and safety hazards of that work. Every employee is also entitled to a daily rest period of 12 consecutive hours and a weekly rest period of 36.

Basic Conditions of Employment Act 75 of 1997, sections 15 and 17. A bargaining council agreement for your sector may vary these conditions where it applies.

In the courts

Asleep on duty, on the record.

Private security has been the CCMA's busiest sector, or joint busiest, for three years running. These four judgments show what decides a guarding dismissal once it gets there.

  1. Labour Court · 2025

    EmployerWonon review

    One blurry photo.

    What happened
    A supervisor said he found a guard asleep in the guardhouse and photographed her. The photo was blurry, and she denied it was her.
    What decided it
    The arbitrator found the charge unproven. On review in March 2025, the Labour Court found the dismissal fair: the employer won, but only after losing the first round.
    What a reviewed record adds
    A clear, timestamped clip of the post, checked by a reviewer the same night, rather than one photo taken in the moment.
    Judgment: [2025] ZALCJHB 130

  2. Labour Court · 2025

    CaseSent back

    Eight final warnings.

    What happened
    Two officers left their post, drove to a quiet spot and were found after sleeping for about two hours. Both were dismissed.
    What decided it
    Their union showed eight other cases of officers caught asleep who had mostly been given final warnings, and the CCMA reinstated the pair for inconsistency. In January 2025 the Labour Court set that aside and sent the case back for a fresh hearing.
    What a reviewed record adds
    Every breach logged the same way, so each sanction can be compared with the cases before it, and any difference explained.
    Judgment: [2025] ZALCJHB 17

  3. Labour Court · 2024

    EmployeeCleared

    The puddle.

    What happened
    An officer driving an escort vehicle skidded off the highway and was dismissed over the crash.
    What decided it
    Having seen the video and photos, the commissioner found it plausible that he hit standing water, and 107 km/h in a 120 zone reasonable while keeping pace with the client. Reinstated with back pay; the employer's review was dismissed.
    What a reviewed record adds
    The same footage that proves misconduct can clear an officer just as quickly, ideally before anyone is dismissed.
    Judgment: [2024] ZALCD 26

  4. CCMA award · 2021

    EmployerLost

    A test is not a hearing.

    What happened
    Stock was going missing at a supermarket guarded by 20 officers. All of them sat a lie detector test, and one was dismissed for “failing” it.
    What decided it
    The commissioner held that the test alone couldn't justify a dismissal, which needed a hearing and corroborating evidence. Unfair: 12 months' pay, R56,400, plus interest.
    What a reviewed record adds
    Footage of what actually happened at the post: the kind of corroboration a polygraph result can't provide on its own.
    Report: GroundUp, 2021

The lessonWhat decided these cases wasn't whether there was a camera or a test. It was whether the evidence was clear, complete and applied the same way to everyone.

Security questions

All questions
Does SIG Eagle Eye replace our control room?

No. Your controllers still run the site. Eagle Eye adds a consistent, classified record of what happened at each post, and every flag is reviewed by a person, so controllers spend less time scrubbing through footage and more time responding.

Can it show that our patrols were actually walked?

Where the route is covered by cameras, the record shows whether it was walked, when, and how long it took. What can be linked to your existing patrol or guard-tour system is scoped at the site assessment.

We guard our clients' sites, not our own. Can we still use it?

Yes, with the client's agreement. Notices, who may access records and how long they are kept are agreed in writing with the site owner before anything goes live.

Will our officers know about it?

They must. Transparent notification is one of the built-in safeguards. Officers who do the job properly usually welcome a record that backs them up. Our page for employees explains it in plain language.
